# Xaman and Ripple

### Background

People often ask whether Xaman belongs to Ripple, and it is easy to see why. Both companies are deeply involved in the XRP Ledger (XRPL), and they have worked together as partners for years. The short answer is no: Xaman is built by XRPL Labs, an independent, privately owned company, and Ripple does not own or run it. This page explains how the two companies are connected, and why the answer matters less for your funds than you might expect. It applies to Xaman on both the XRP Ledger and Xahau.

### Who owns Xaman

XRPL Labs builds and runs Xaman, and it does not share ownership with Ripple. Like most companies in the XRP Ledger community, XRPL Labs works with many partners: other companies, organizations, and independent developers. Ripple is one of those partners.

Ripple did help XRPL Labs get started. In 2019, Xpring, Ripple's developer initiative at the time (now RippleX), funded the company so its team could build XRP Ledger tools full time, and it added more funding in 2020. That funding did not give Ripple ownership of XRPL Labs or any control over Xaman. Ripple itself said at the time that the team would choose its own projects independently.

### Ripple, XRP, and the XRP Ledger

A lot of the confusion comes from mixing up three different things. Ripple is a company. The XRP Ledger is a public network, run by independent computers around the world, and XRP is its native coin. Ripple uses the XRP Ledger and contributes to it, but the network does not belong to Ripple or to any other company.

Xaman connects you to the XRP Ledger itself. Your account lives there, not with Ripple and not with XRPL Labs, which is why the question of who owns which company never changes who controls your account.

### What this means for your funds

Xaman is a self-custody[^1] wallet, so your [account secret](#user-content-fn-2)[^2] stays on your phone or your Xaman card, and only you can sign for your account. Neither XRPL Labs nor Ripple can move, freeze, or recover your XRP, and neither can reverse a transaction once the network has confirmed it.

There is one place where Ripple does have a say: tokens that Ripple issues itself, such as the RLUSD stablecoin. As the token's issuer[^3], Ripple set up RLUSD so that it can freeze the token or take it back. Ripple has explained that these powers exist so RLUSD balances can follow its legal obligations, such as court orders. This applies only to Ripple's own tokens, never to your XRP. _Token freezes and clawback_ explains how these issuer powers work.

### Frequently asked questions

**Is Xaman Ripple's wallet?**

No. Xaman is made by XRPL Labs, a separate company. Ripple is a partner, not an owner, and it has no access to your Xaman account.

**What is a "Ripple account"?**

It is an older name for an XRP Ledger account. The XRP Ledger was first called Ripple, so early wallets and guides spoke of "Ripple accounts", "Ripple addresses", and "Ripple wallets", and you may still see those names today. They all mean the same thing as your XRP Ledger account, the one with your r-address. You do not need to sign up with Ripple, the company, to use Xaman.

**Someone from Ripple contacted me about my Xaman account. Is that real?**

No. Ripple has no access to your Xaman account and no part in supporting it. The only official way to reach Xaman support is the Xaman Support xApp, inside the app. Our support team will never ask for your account secret. Anyone who asks for it is trying to take your funds, so never share it.

**If something happened to Ripple, would Xaman stop working?**

No. Xaman is built by XRPL Labs, and your account lives on the XRP Ledger, which does not depend on any single company. _What happens to my account if Xaman shuts down?_ explains how your access works in more detail.

**Does Ripple control the XRP Ledger?**

No. Ripple uses the XRP Ledger and contributes to it, but the network is run by independent computers around the world, and no single company controls it.
