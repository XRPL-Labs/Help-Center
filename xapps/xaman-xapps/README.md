# Xaman xApps

xApps[^1] add features to Xaman without making the app itself bigger. Some are made by XRPL Labs, the team behind Xaman, and many more come from other developers on the XRP Ledger (XRPL). This page lists the ones XRPL Labs makes, with a line on what each one does, so you can find the right one quickly.

No xApp can move anything on its own. When an xApp wants to send a payment or change a setting, it sends a [sign request](#user-content-fn-2)[^2] to Xaman, and nothing happens until you review it and slide to accept. Read every request from an xApp as carefully as one from a website, whoever made it.

### Open an xApp

Tap **xApps** in the bottom navigation bar to see every xApp in Xaman. The **Home** tab shows **Our suggestions** and **Popular**, and **Recent**, **A-Z**, and **Categories** help you find one by use, name, or type. To search, tap the search icon at the top. Tap an xApp's info icon to read what it does before you open it.

### xApps from other developers

Most xApps in the directory come from other teams building on the XRP Ledger. Some are listed under [XRPL Services](https://help.xaman.app/app/xapps/xrpl-services) and [XRP Community](https://help.xaman.app/app/xapps/xrp-community) in this Help Center. They can be useful, but XRPL Labs does not run them, so their own teams answer questions about them. [Trust in the XRPL community](https://help.xaman.app/app/scams-and-staying-safe/trust-in-the-xrpl-community) explains how to decide which ones to trust.

Crypto assets can change in value. This article explains how the feature works and is not financial advice.
