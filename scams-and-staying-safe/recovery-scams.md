# Recovery scams

Losing crypto to a scam or a theft is hard enough. Unfortunately, it can also make you a target for a second scam, often within days. Recovery scammers contact people who have just lost funds and promise to get them back, for a fee. They never recover anything, and the money paid to them is lost too.

This article explains how recovery scams work, the signs that give them away, and what real help looks like. If you have already been in contact with someone like this, it also covers what to do now. Knowing how these scams work is the best protection, since they rely on reaching you at a moment when you badly want to believe them.

Xaman is a self-custody wallet. A confirmed XRP Ledger transaction cannot be reversed, by Xaman, by the network, or by anyone else. No service, investigator, or hacker can pull your XRP back from the account it was sent to.

### How recovery scams work

Recovery scammers watch for people who have lost crypto. They find them in comments on social media, in posts asking for help, in community forums, and sometimes in lists of past scam victims. Then they reach out, usually within hours or days, with an offer that sounds like exactly what you hoped to hear.

They present themselves as people who can help. Common disguises include:

* A "blockchain recovery" company or "crypto investigator".
* A police officer, the FBI, Interpol, or another government agency.
* A law firm offering to file a claim on your behalf.
* An "ethical hacker" who says they can break into the scammer's account.
* Someone claiming to work for Xaman, XRPL Labs, Ripple, or an exchange.

The story usually goes well at first. They may show you a report about your stolen funds, or even a screenshot that seems to prove they have found them. Then the requests start: an upfront fee, a "tax" or "release fee" before the funds can be returned, your account secret, or access to your phone or computer. Each payment is followed by another request, until the victim stops paying.

### The warning signs

Any one of these signs means you are dealing with a scammer:

* **They contacted you first.** Real investigators rarely reach out unprompted through social media or messaging apps.
* **They ask for money up front.** This includes fees, deposits, taxes, or "gas" payments before anything is returned.
* **They ask for your account secret,** or for access to your phone or computer through a screen-sharing or remote-access app.
* **They promise to reverse a transaction.** Confirmed XRP Ledger transactions cannot be reversed by anyone.
* **They claim to work with Xaman or XRPL Labs.** Xaman does not run, partner with, or recommend any recovery service.
* **They are in a hurry.** They say the funds will be lost forever unless you act today.

{% hint style="danger" %}
**No one** can recover your funds by asking for your account secret or an upfront fee. Anyone who asks for either will take whatever you give them. Do not pay, and do not share your account secret with anyone.
{% endhint %}

### What real help looks like

Real help never costs a fee, and it never needs your account secret. The police and national cybercrime agencies do not charge victims to investigate. Xaman support does not charge for help either, and it never asks for your account secret.

In some cases, stolen funds can be frozen. This happens when they land at an exchange that knows its customers' identities, and the police ask the exchange to act. That process runs between the police and the exchange, never through a private service that contacts you. The most useful thing you can do is report the theft to your local police or national cybercrime authority, with your r-address and the transaction details.

If someone says they are from the police or a government agency, check before you share anything. Look up the agency's phone number yourself, from its official website, and call to confirm. Do not use a phone number, email address, or link that the person gave you.

### If someone offers to recover your funds

If someone contacts you with an offer to get your crypto back, follow these steps:

1. Stop replying to them.
2. Do not pay anything, and do not install any app they suggest.
3. Keep a copy of the messages, including names, phone numbers, and addresses.
4. Report them to your local police or national cybercrime authority.
5. Block them on the platform they used to contact you.

If you are unsure whether a message is genuine, ask us first, through the [Xaman Support xApp](https://xaman.app/detect/xapp:xumm.support-md).&#x20;

### If you already paid or shared information

If this has happened to you, you are not alone. Recovery scammers are practiced at sounding convincing, and they reach people at the moment they most want to believe them. What matters now is limiting the damage, so act on whichever of these applies to you.

* **If you paid a fee,** do not pay anything more, whatever reason they give. Report the payment to the police, together with the original theft.
* **If you shared your account secret,** treat your account as no longer yours to control. Move anything left in it to a safe account, or rekey your account, right away. [Account present on another device](../security/all-about-security/account-present-on-another-device.md) walks you through both options.
* **If you installed an app they asked for,** remove it from your phone or computer. Then follow the step above for your account secret, since the app may have shown them your screen.

### Frequently asked questions

**Can anyone really reverse an XRP Ledger transaction?**

No. Once a transaction is confirmed, it is permanent. Neither Xaman, the XRP Ledger, nor any service can reverse it. See [Can Xaman reverse, freeze or undo a transaction?](../sending-receiving-and-cashing-out/can-xaman-reverse-freeze-or-undo-a-transaction.md)

**The person says they work with Xaman. Could that be true?**

No. Xaman does not work with any recovery service, and we will never contact you offering to recover funds. If in doubt, contact us through the [Xaman Support xApp](https://xaman.app/detect/xapp:xumm.support-md).

**I posted about my loss online, and now strangers are messaging me. What should I do?**

Treat every unexpected offer of help as a likely scam, however kind or professional it sounds. Do not reply to these messages, and consider removing your post. Most people who send them are recovery scammers who found you through it.

**What if the police really do contact me?**

That can happen, especially if you reported the theft. Confirm the contact by calling the agency on a number from its official website. A genuine officer will never ask you for a fee, your account secret, or access to your phone or computer.
